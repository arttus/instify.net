# Legal Terminology Glossary

## A

**Acquittal**: A finding of not guilty in a criminal trial. The defendant is released and cannot be tried again for the same offense due to double jeopardy protections.

**Admissible Evidence**: Evidence that can be presented in court because it meets legal standards of relevance and reliability. Inadmissible evidence is excluded by the judge.

**Affidavit**: A written statement made under oath. The person making the affidavit (affiant) swears the contents are true and can face perjury charges for lying.

**Affirmative Defense**: A defense strategy that introduces new facts showing the defendant is not liable, even if the plaintiff's allegations are true (e.g., self-defense, statute of limitations).

**Alimony (Spousal Support/Maintenance)**: Financial support paid by one spouse to another during separation or after divorce. Can be temporary, rehabilitative, or permanent depending on circumstances.

**Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)**: Methods of resolving disputes outside of court, including mediation, arbitration, and negotiation. Often faster and less expensive than litigation.

**Answer**: The defendant's formal written response to a complaint, admitting or denying allegations and raising defenses.

**Appeal**: Request for a higher court to review a lower court's decision. Appeals focus on legal errors, not re-examining facts.

**Appellant**: The party who files an appeal (typically the losing party at trial).

**Appellee**: The party responding to an appeal (typically the winner at trial).

**Arbitration**: A form of ADR where a neutral arbitrator hears evidence and makes a binding decision. More formal than mediation but less formal than trial.

**Arraignment**: Initial court appearance in criminal case where defendant hears charges, enters a plea, and bail is set.

**Arrest Warrant**: Court order authorizing law enforcement to arrest a specific person based on probable cause that they committed a crime.

**Asset**: Property owned by a person or entity that has value. Includes real estate, vehicles, bank accounts, investments, businesses, and personal property.

**At-Will Employment**: Employment that can be terminated by either employer or employee at any time for any legal reason or no reason, without advance notice.

**Attorney-Client Privilege**: Legal protection preventing attorneys from disclosing confidential communications with clients. Encourages open and honest communication.

## B

**Bail**: Money or property deposited with the court to ensure a defendant's appearance at trial. Returned when defendant appears; forfeited if defendant flees.

**Bench Trial**: Trial decided by a judge without a jury.

**Beneficiary**: Person or entity designated to receive benefits, assets, or property (e.g., from a will, trust, or insurance policy).

**Beyond a Reasonable Doubt**: The standard of proof in criminal cases. The prosecution must prove guilt to such a degree that a reasonable person would have no reasonable doubt.

**Breach of Contract**: Failure to perform obligations under a contract without legal excuse. Can be material (serious) or minor.

**Brief**: Written legal argument submitted to a court explaining a party's position on legal issues, with supporting law and facts.

**Burden of Proof**: The obligation to prove allegations or defenses. In civil cases: preponderance of evidence. In criminal cases: beyond reasonable doubt.

## C

**Capital Crime**: A crime punishable by death or life imprisonment.

**Case Law**: Law established by previous court decisions (precedents). Judges follow precedents from higher courts in their jurisdiction.

**Cause of Action**: The legal basis for a lawsuit. The facts and law supporting a claim (e.g., breach of contract, negligence, fraud).

**Cease and Desist Letter**: Letter demanding that a party stop allegedly illegal activity. Often precedes lawsuit and may settle disputes.

**Change of Venue**: Moving a case to a different court location, often due to publicity making fair trial impossible or for convenience.

**Child Custody**: Legal determination of where a child lives and who makes decisions about the child's upbringing. Includes legal custody (decision-making) and physical custody (residence).

**Child Support**: Monetary support paid by non-custodial parent to custodial parent for child's expenses. Based on state guidelines considering parental income and number of children.

**Civil Case**: Lawsuit between private parties (individuals, businesses, organizations) seeking money damages or specific actions. Different from criminal cases.

**Class Action**: Lawsuit filed by representative plaintiffs on behalf of a large group of people with similar claims against the same defendant.

**Closing Statement (Closing Argument)**: Final presentation to judge or jury at end of trial, summarizing evidence and arguing why party should win.

**Codicil**: An amendment to a will that adds to, changes, or revokes provisions without redrafting the entire will.

**Common Law**: Law developed through court decisions rather than statutes. Also called case law or judge-made law.

**Compensatory Damages**: Money awarded to compensate plaintiff for actual losses (medical bills, lost wages, property damage). Intended to make plaintiff whole.

**Complaint**: The initial document filed to start a lawsuit, stating the facts, legal claims, and relief requested.

**Confidentiality Agreement (Non-Disclosure Agreement/NDA)**: Contract where parties agree to keep certain information private and not disclose it to third parties.

**Conflict of Interest**: Situation where attorney's or party's interests conflict with duties to client or another party. Attorneys cannot represent clients with conflicting interests.

**Consideration**: Something of value exchanged in a contract (money, services, promises, goods). Essential element of enforceable contracts.

**Contempt of Court**: Willful disobedience of court orders or disrespectful behavior in court. Can result in fines or jail time.

**Contingency Fee**: Fee arrangement where attorney receives percentage of recovery only if case is won. No recovery = no fee.

**Continuance**: Postponement of a court hearing or trial to a later date, granted by the court.

**Contract**: Legally enforceable agreement between two or more parties involving exchange of promises or actions.

**Copyright**: Exclusive legal right to reproduce, distribute, and display original creative works (books, music, art, software).

**Counterclaim**: Claim made by defendant against plaintiff in the same lawsuit, arising from the same transaction or occurrence.

**Court Order**: Direction issued by a judge requiring a party to do or refrain from doing something. Enforceable through contempt.

**Cross-Examination**: Questioning of a witness by the opposing party after direct examination, attempting to challenge credibility or clarify testimony.

## D

**Damages**: Monetary compensation awarded to a party who suffered loss or injury. Types include compensatory, punitive, nominal, and liquidated.

**Default Judgment**: Judgment entered against a defendant who fails to appear or respond to a lawsuit.

**Defendant**: The party being sued in a civil case or accused in a criminal case.

**Deposition**: Out-of-court oral testimony given under oath, with questions by attorneys and answers recorded by court reporter. Part of discovery.

**Discovery**: Pre-trial process where parties exchange information and evidence. Includes depositions, interrogatories, requests for documents, and admissions.

**Dismissal**: Court's decision to terminate a case. Can be with prejudice (cannot be refiled) or without prejudice (can be refiled).

**Diversity Jurisdiction**: Federal court jurisdiction over cases between citizens of different states where amount exceeds $75,000.

**Docket**: Court's calendar of scheduled cases; also the official record of proceedings in a case.

**Double Jeopardy**: Constitutional protection preventing a person from being tried twice for the same criminal offense.

**Due Process**: Constitutional guarantee of fair legal procedures before government can deprive someone of life, liberty, or property.

**Durable Power of Attorney**: Document allowing someone to make financial and legal decisions on your behalf, continuing even if you become incapacitated.

## E

**Emancipation**: Legal process by which a minor becomes independent from parents before reaching age of majority.

**Eminent Domain**: Government's power to take private property for public use with fair compensation.

**Entitlement**: Right to receive a benefit or payment (e.g., Social Security, inheritance).

**Escrow**: Money or property held by neutral third party until conditions are met (common in real estate transactions).

**Estate**: All property and assets owned by a person at death. Also refers to property held in trust or bankruptcy estate.

**Estate Planning**: Process of arranging for management and distribution of assets during life and after death through wills, trusts, and other documents.

**Evidence**: Information presented in court to prove or disprove facts. Includes testimony, documents, physical objects, and demonstrative evidence.

**Ex Parte**: Legal proceeding involving only one party without notice to the other party. Used only in emergencies or specific circumstances.

**Executor (Personal Representative)**: Person named in will to manage estate, pay debts, and distribute assets. Appointed by court.

**Expert Witness**: Person with specialized knowledge, skill, education, or experience who testifies to help judge or jury understand complex issues.

**Expungement**: Court-ordered sealing or destruction of criminal records. Makes it as if the arrest or conviction never occurred.

## F

**Federal Question Jurisdiction**: Federal court jurisdiction over cases involving federal laws, U.S. Constitution, or treaties.

**Felony**: Serious crime punishable by more than one year in prison. More severe than misdemeanor.

**Fiduciary**: Person who holds position of trust and must act in another's best interest (e.g., trustee, executor, attorney, guardian).

**Filing Fee**: Fee paid to court clerk to file legal documents like complaints, motions, or appeals.

**Foreclosure**: Legal process by which lender takes possession of property when borrower fails to make mortgage payments.

**Forum Shopping**: Attempting to have case heard in jurisdiction likely to provide favorable outcome.

**Fraud**: Intentional misrepresentation of material fact that another person relies on to their detriment.

**Full Faith and Credit**: Constitutional provision requiring states to recognize other states' court judgments and public records.

## G

**Garnishment**: Court order requiring third party (usually employer) to withhold money from defendant's wages or accounts to pay judgment creditor.

**Good Faith**: Honest intention to act without deception or unfair advantage. Required in many contracts and legal relationships.

**Grand Jury**: Group of citizens who hear evidence and determine whether sufficient probable cause exists to indict someone for a crime.

**Green Card**: Permanent Resident Card allowing non-citizens to live and work permanently in the United States.

**Guardian**: Person appointed by court to care for minor or incapacitated adult and/or manage their property.

**Guardian ad Litem (GAL)**: Attorney or trained professional appointed to represent child's best interests in custody or abuse cases.

## H

**Habeas Corpus**: Writ requiring person holding another to bring that person before court to determine if detention is lawful. Protects against unlawful imprisonment.

**Hearing**: Court proceeding before a judge (typically without jury) to resolve specific issues or make determinations.

**Hearsay**: Out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. Generally inadmissible unless exception applies.

**Hung Jury**: Jury that cannot reach unanimous verdict (or required majority), resulting in mistrial.

## I

**Immunity**: Legal protection from liability or prosecution. Types include sovereign immunity, diplomatic immunity, and witness immunity.

**Impeachment**: Challenging the credibility of a witness through cross-examination or other evidence.

**Inadmissible**: Evidence that cannot be presented to the jury, usually because it violates rules of evidence.

**Incapacitated**: Unable to make or communicate informed decisions about health, finances, or personal matters due to mental or physical condition.

**Indictment**: Formal charging document issued by grand jury stating there is probable cause that defendant committed a crime.

**Injunction**: Court order requiring someone to do or stop doing a specific act. Can be temporary or permanent.

**Intellectual Property**: Creations of the mind protected by law, including copyrights, patents, trademarks, and trade secrets.

**Interrogatories**: Written questions sent by one party to another that must be answered under oath. Part of discovery.

**Intestate**: Dying without a valid will. Assets distributed according to state intestacy laws.

**Irrevocable Trust**: Trust that cannot be changed or terminated by the creator. Offers asset protection and tax benefits but requires giving up control.

## J

**Joint Custody**: Arrangement where both parents share legal custody (decision-making) and/or physical custody (residence time) of children.

**Joint Tenancy**: Property ownership by two or more people with right of survivorship (property automatically passes to surviving owner(s) at death).

**Judgment**: Court's final decision on the rights and obligations of parties in a case. Can be entered after trial or by default.

**Jurisdiction**: Court's authority to hear and decide a case. Based on subject matter, geographic location, and parties involved.

**Jury Instructions**: Judge's explanation to jury of the law applicable to the case and how to apply it to the facts.

**Jury Trial**: Trial where jury decides factual questions and applies law as instructed by judge.

## L

**Lawsuit (Litigation)**: Legal action in court to enforce rights or seek remedies.

**Leading Question**: Question that suggests the desired answer. Generally not allowed on direct examination but permitted on cross-examination.

**Legal Separation**: Court-ordered arrangement where married couple lives apart but remains legally married. Addresses custody, support, and property issues.

**Liability**: Legal responsibility for harm or damages. Can be based on negligence, strict liability, or intentional conduct.

**Lien**: Legal claim against property to secure payment of debt. Property cannot be sold/transferred until lien is satisfied.

**Limited Liability Company (LLC)**: Business entity providing liability protection to owners while allowing flexible management and pass-through taxation.

**Litigation Hold**: Duty to preserve relevant documents and information when litigation is anticipated or pending.

**Living Trust**: Trust created during lifetime that holds assets for benefit of creator during life and distributes them after death. Avoids probate.

**Living Will (Advance Directive)**: Document stating wishes for medical treatment if unable to make decisions. Separate from regular will.

## M

**Malpractice**: Professional negligence by attorney, doctor, accountant, or other professional that harms client/patient.

**Mandatory Settlement Conference**: Court-ordered meeting where parties attempt to settle case before trial with help of judge or mediator.

**Marital Property**: Assets and debts acquired during marriage. Subject to division in divorce (rules vary by state).

**Material Fact**: Fact important enough to affect a decision. Misrepresentation of material fact can void contracts or constitute fraud.

**Mediation**: ADR process where neutral mediator helps parties negotiate settlement. Non-binding unless parties reach agreement.

**Miranda Rights**: Constitutional rights that must be read to suspects before custodial interrogation (right to remain silent, right to attorney).

**Misdemeanor**: Crime less serious than felony, typically punishable by fine or up to one year in county jail.

**Mistrial**: Trial ending without verdict due to error, jury deadlock, or other problem. Case may be retried.

**Motion**: Formal request to court for ruling or order. Can be written or oral depending on court rules and situation.

**Motion for Summary Judgment**: Request for judgment without trial because there are no genuine disputes of material fact.

**Motion in Limine**: Pre-trial motion seeking to exclude or include certain evidence at trial.

**Motion to Dismiss**: Request to terminate case due to legal deficiencies (improper jurisdiction, failure to state claim, etc.).

## N

**Negligence**: Failure to exercise reasonable care, resulting in harm to another. Elements: duty, breach, causation, damages.

**No-Fault Divorce**: Divorce that doesn't require proving wrongdoing by either spouse. Based on irreconcilable differences or irretrievable breakdown.

**Non-Compete Agreement**: Contract restricting employee's ability to work for competitors or start competing business after leaving employment.

**Notary Public**: Official authorized to witness signatures, administer oaths, and certify documents.

**Notice**: Formal notification to parties of legal proceedings, hearings, or actions. Must meet specific legal requirements.

## O

**Objection**: Formal protest by attorney to improper question, evidence, or procedure. Judge rules whether objection is sustained or overruled.

**Opening Statement**: Attorney's initial presentation to judge or jury outlining what evidence will show. Not evidence or argument.

**Order**: Written command from judge directing parties to do or refrain from doing something.

**Overrule**: Judge's rejection of attorney's objection or lower court's decision.

## P

**Parole**: Supervised release from prison before full sentence served. Violation can result in return to prison.

**Patent**: Exclusive right to make, use, and sell an invention for limited time (typically 20 years).

**Personal Jurisdiction**: Court's authority over parties to a case, based on their connections to the state.

**Petition**: Formal written request to court for specific relief or action.

**Plaintiff**: Party who files lawsuit seeking relief.

**Plea Bargain**: Agreement between prosecution and defendant where defendant pleads guilty to lesser charge or for reduced sentence.

**Pleadings**: Formal documents filed in lawsuit stating parties' claims, defenses, and positions (complaint, answer, counterclaim).

**Power of Attorney**: Document authorizing someone to act on your behalf for legal and financial matters.

**Precedent**: Previous court decision that guides decisions in similar future cases. Binding precedent must be followed.

**Preponderance of Evidence**: Standard of proof in civil cases. More likely true than not true (greater than 50% probability).

**Prima Facie Case**: Case with sufficient evidence to proceed to trial and potentially win if not rebutted by defendant.

**Privilege**: Legal right to refuse to disclose information (attorney-client, doctor-patient, spousal, etc.).

**Probable Cause**: Reasonable belief, based on facts, that a crime was committed or that person committed it. Required for arrest or search warrant.

**Probate**: Court process of validating will, paying debts, and distributing deceased person's assets.

**Probation**: Sentence allowing convicted person to remain in community under supervision instead of imprisonment.

**Pro Bono**: Legal services provided free of charge, typically to those who cannot afford an attorney.

**Process Server**: Person who delivers legal documents to parties in lawsuit.

**Protective Order (Restraining Order)**: Court order prohibiting person from contacting, approaching, or harming another person.

**Punitive Damages**: Money awarded to punish defendant for egregious conduct and deter similar behavior. Beyond compensation for actual harm.

## Q

**Quid Pro Quo**: "Something for something." Exchange of acts or things of approximately equal value (consideration in contracts).

**Quiet Title**: Lawsuit to establish ownership of real property and eliminate competing claims.

## R

**Reasonable Doubt**: Standard of proof in criminal cases. Doubt that would cause reasonable person to hesitate before acting on important matters.

**Recuse**: Judge's voluntary withdrawal from case due to conflict of interest or bias.

**Remand**: Appellate court sending case back to trial court for further proceedings.

**Res Judicata**: Doctrine preventing relitigation of claims already decided. "Matter already judged."

**Respondent**: Party responding to petition or appeal.

**Restitution**: Court-ordered payment by defendant to victim to compensate for losses.

**Retainer**: Fee paid upfront to secure attorney's services. May be advance payment for future work or non-refundable fee for availability.

**Reverse**: Appellate court overturning lower court's decision.

**Revocable Trust**: Trust that creator can change or terminate during lifetime. Provides flexibility and probate avoidance.

**Right of First Refusal**: Right to purchase property or match competing offer before owner sells to someone else.

## S

**Search Warrant**: Court order authorizing law enforcement to search specific location for specific items.

**Self-Defense**: Justified use of force to protect oneself from imminent harm. Affirmative defense to assault charges.

**Separate Property**: Property owned before marriage or acquired during marriage by gift or inheritance. Not subject to division in divorce (in most states).

**Service of Process**: Formal delivery of legal documents to party, providing notice of lawsuit and opportunity to respond.

**Settlement**: Agreement between parties to resolve dispute without trial. Can occur at any stage of litigation.

**Sole Proprietorship**: Unincorporated business owned by one person. No liability protection; owner personally liable for debts.

**Specific Performance**: Court order requiring party to perform contractual obligations. Alternative to money damages when unique subject matter (e.g., real estate).

**Spoliation**: Destruction, alteration, or concealment of evidence. Can result in sanctions or adverse inference.

**Standard of Proof**: Level of certainty required to prove case. Varies by type of case (preponderance, clear and convincing, beyond reasonable doubt).

**Standing**: Legal right to bring lawsuit. Plaintiff must have suffered actual injury caused by defendant.

**Statute**: Law enacted by legislature.

**Statute of Limitations**: Time limit for filing lawsuit. Varies by claim type and state.

**Stay**: Court order temporarily suspending proceedings or enforcement of judgment.

**Stipulation**: Agreement between parties about procedural or factual matters, reducing issues in dispute.

**Strict Liability**: Liability without proving negligence or fault. Applied to inherently dangerous activities and product defects.

**Subpoena**: Court order requiring person to testify or produce documents. Failure to comply can result in contempt.

**Summary Judgment**: Judgment without trial when no genuine factual disputes exist and moving party entitled to judgment as matter of law.

**Summons**: Document notifying defendant of lawsuit and requiring response within specified time.

## T

**Testamentary Trust**: Trust created by will, taking effect upon death.

**Testator**: Person who makes a will.

**Testimony**: Oral statements made under oath during depositions or trial.

**Title**: Legal ownership of property.

**Title Insurance**: Insurance protecting property owner and lender against title defects, liens, or ownership disputes.

**Tort**: Civil wrong (other than breach of contract) causing harm for which courts provide remedy. Includes negligence, battery, defamation.

**Trademark**: Symbol, word, or phrase identifying and distinguishing source of goods or services.

**Trial**: Formal court proceeding where evidence is presented and judge or jury decides case.

**Trial Court**: Court where case is initially heard and evidence presented (as opposed to appellate court).

**Trust**: Legal arrangement where trustee holds property for benefit of beneficiaries according to trust terms.

**Trustee**: Person or entity managing trust assets for benefit of beneficiaries. Has fiduciary duty to act in beneficiaries' best interests.

## U

**Uncontested Divorce**: Divorce where spouses agree on all issues (property, custody, support). Faster and less expensive than contested divorce.

**Undue Influence**: Excessive pressure causing person to act against their own interests or free will. Can invalidate wills, contracts, or other legal documents.

**Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)**: Standardized laws governing commercial transactions, adopted by all states with variations.

## V

**Venue**: Geographic location where case is heard. Based on where events occurred or where parties reside.

**Verdict**: Jury's decision on facts in case.

**Void**: Invalid and unenforceable from the beginning. Has no legal effect.

**Voidable**: Valid until party exercises right to void it (e.g., contract signed under duress).

**Voir Dire**: Process of questioning prospective jurors to determine qualifications and identify bias.

## W

**Waiver**: Voluntary and intentional giving up of a known right.

**Ward**: Person (usually minor or incapacitated adult) under protection of court-appointed guardian.

**Warrant**: Court order authorizing law enforcement action (arrest, search, seizure).

**Will**: Legal document directing distribution of person's property after death. Must meet state formality requirements.

**Witness**: Person who testifies in court or gives deposition. Can be lay witness (personal knowledge) or expert witness (specialized knowledge).

**Writ**: Formal written court order directing person or entity to do or refrain from doing specified act.

**Wrongful Death**: Death caused by negligent, reckless, or intentional conduct of another. Survivors can sue for damages.

## Z

**Zoning**: Government regulations controlling land use in specific geographic areas. Restricts types of buildings, businesses, and activities.

---

*This glossary provides general definitions. Legal terms may have specific meanings depending on jurisdiction and context. Consult an attorney for precise definitions applicable to your situation.*
